GrüvType — Independent Documentary / Publishing

We document
worlds.

We enter worlds while their people, places, objects, knowledge and working practices can still be found—and create durable documentary records through research, field reporting, photography, sound, moving image, archives and books.

The Practice

Some worlds disappear.
We document them while they are here.

A garden. A countertop machine. A boat. A room. A bottle of wine. An American-made chair. An axe. A garment. A house. A place where something is made.

We look for worlds that already exist around the things people encounter, value, use or depend upon—but whose histories, materials, techniques, people and accumulated knowledge are rarely preserved as a whole.

Some of these worlds are stable. Some are changing. Some are already disappearing.

We enter the world, investigate it, document it and create a durable record. The book is one of the principal physical embodiments of that record. Organizations that possess, steward or inhabit these worlds may become partners in their documentation.

Editorial Position

A tradition of
looking closely.

GrüvType belongs to a tradition of publishing that treated the ordinary world as worthy of serious attention. People at work. Places as they actually are. Objects, materials and processes seen at close range.

Not as advertising. Not as decoration. As evidence.

The Difference

The record
comes first.

Subject first. We begin with a world worth understanding, then identify the people, organizations, places, objects, systems and knowledge that belong to it.
Not advertising. Companies and institutions may belong to a documented world, but their commercial relationship to the work does not determine the editorial subject, argument or outcome.
Not a catalog. The purpose is not to display products. It is to understand the larger world in which those products, people, places and practices exist.
A permanent record. Research, fieldwork, photography, interviews, archives, objects, places, processes and knowledge are brought together into a record that can continue to be useful after the moment of documentation has passed.
What Makes a GrüvType Subject

You already know it.
You just don't know the whole world.

The best subjects begin with familiar territory and open into a much larger documentary world. We look for subjects with enough depth, evidence and consequence to deserve preservation.

Existing

A real world already inhabited by people, objects, places, systems, knowledge or working practices.

Vulnerable

Useful

For Companies & Institutions

The documentation
is the work.

A commissioned or institutional documentation project creates a defined body of research, fieldwork, photography, interviews, archival material and documentary assets around a company, institution, place, process or specialized world.

A substantial book may become the principal published expression of that work. But the underlying record can have value independently of the publication—as institutional history, archive, reference, teaching material, exhibition material, customer or dealer resource, or a permanent record of knowledge that might otherwise become scattered or disappear.

The Record Research, fieldwork, photography, interviews, archival material and original documentation of the world itself: its people, places, objects, processes, history and knowledge.
The Publication A substantial physical expression of the record, designed to be read, kept, circulated, archived and used as institutional history, reference, teaching material, exhibition object, customer resource or dealer resource.
The Documentary Archive Original photography, interviews, audio, moving image, maps, archival captures and other documentary material created in the course of the work. Selected materials may be delivered for agreed institutional use or licensed separately.
Institutional Memory The record can preserve knowledge that is otherwise scattered across people, facilities, machines, manuals, photographs, archives and working practices— particularly where an organization or industry is changing or where knowledge may otherwise be lost.
Continuing Use A serious documentary record can remain useful long after a campaign ends: in archives, education, exhibitions, training, customer relationships, dealer relationships, institutional history and future research.

A partner is not purchasing editorial placement. It is commissioning, acquiring or enabling the documentation of a world in which it has a substantial stake.

The work does not require disclosure of proprietary formulas, confidential processes, customer information or unreleased material. We document what is appropriate to document and publish, and company-supplied material is handled under agreed terms.

The editorial relationship remains independent. Commissioning, acquisition or institutional support does not buy pages, favorable treatment, product placement or control of the finished publication.

The Larger World

What sits
behind the thing.

The object is often only the visible edge of a much larger world. We enter that world and follow the connections until the larger system becomes visible—and record it while the evidence is still there to be found.

Objects The things themselves, examined closely and understood in relation to how they are made and used.
Materials The physical substances that give a world its character.
Making Factories, workshops, kitchens, farms, studios, garages, nurseries and the people who know how things actually come into being.
Places Buildings, rooms, landscapes, workplaces and environments where the subject becomes real.
Design Decisions about form, function, proportion, material and use.
Knowledge Vocabulary, techniques, traditions, inventions and accumulated expertise.
Editorial Method

We follow the thing
until the world appears.

This is field reporting: research first. Then fieldwork. Then the objects, people, photographs, documents and unexpected connections that make the subject legible. The goal is not simply to describe the present, but to preserve evidence of how a world came to be and how it actually works.

Research History, terminology, chronology, archives and primary sources.
Fieldwork Workshops, factories, kitchens, vineyards, ateliers, garages, nurseries, farms and other places where the subject exists.
Objects Tools, machines, materials, packaging and finished things as physical evidence.
Photography Original documentary photography showing things as they are made, used, maintained and handled.
Primary Evidence Manuals, catalogs, labels, drawings, photographs, documents and surviving records.
Knowledge The people whose expertise cannot be reduced to a specification.
The Unexpected Forgotten techniques, unusual uses, obsolete objects and discoveries that make the familiar unfamiliar again.
Material Worlds

Every product has
a world behind it.

A manufacturer may possess decades of knowledge that never appears on a product page. A designer may work with techniques that disappear from a campaign. A garden may contain centuries of accumulated knowledge that becomes invisible once the landscape is finished.

A company may also be one of the last places where a particular machine, technique, vocabulary or body of knowledge can still be observed in working form.

GrüvType goes looking for that larger world before it becomes harder to find.

The Physical Book

A record you
can hold.

A GrüvType book is a field report you can keep: substantial, illustrated, carefully designed and intended to be read, handled, shelved and returned to.

It can be a reference. An archive. A teaching aid. A customer or dealer resource. An exhibition object. A piece of institutional history. A record for the next generation of people who will want to know how the world worked.

It is not a brochure disguised as a book. It is not a catalog assembled around advertising inventory.

The physical book is where the research, photography, reporting and accumulated knowledge become a permanent object that can outlast the moment in which the documentation was made.

The Record

The record is larger
than the book.

A GrüvType project begins with documentation. Where the subject calls for it, the record may include moving image, sound, interviews, process documentation, archival material, original photography and other forms of evidence.

The book is one expression of that record—the most substantial and permanent one—but not the only one.

The objective is to create something worth keeping because the world it records is worth preserving.
