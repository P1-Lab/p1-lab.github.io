01 — The Proposition
A directory tells you where.
ANTIQUE tells you who and what.

Online listings are useful for discovery. They are not built to preserve the character of a business, the depth of a specialty, the history of a dealer, or the physical world inside a shop.

ANTIQUE is conceived as a three-volume annual North American reference to the businesses, specialties, regions, histories and material worlds through which North America buys, sells, studies and preserves objects with history.

The purpose is practical.

To make a fragmented trade legible. To put the specialist beside the region, the dealer beside the material, and the shop beside the history that gives it meaning.

The shop is part of the record.

06 — Partners

The businesses that keep material history in circulation are part of the record.

ANTIQUE is built around a professional trade. Dealers, specialists, restorers, galleries, shops, appraisers, conservators and institutions may hold important parts of that world—and may also have substantial documentary histories of their own.

ANTIQUE does not sell pages.
It records the trade.

The annual three-volume set is organized by material field and region. Participating businesses are placed within the category or categories that describe their actual work, so the books read as a reference to the trade rather than a sequence of purchased advertisements.

Annual Participation

Trade Record

A standardized annual record for an established dealer, specialist, shop or gallery. The Trade Record establishes the practical reference layer of the three-volume set.

  • Business name, location and website
  • Primary specialties and material fields
  • Established date or concise history where appropriate
  • Editorial description of the dealer's field
  • Placement within the relevant material and regional sections
  • Appropriate imagery, properly licensed and editorially selected
  • Inclusion in the annual index
  • A copy of the published annual set
Trade Records do not purchase a page, position, endorsement or advertising space. ANTIQUE determines category, sequence, wording and image treatment as part of the editorial structure of the books.

07 — Method

Follow the object until the world around it appears.

The record moves from physical evidence to provenance, from provenance to people and places, and from individual objects to the larger systems through which the trade operates.

Research establishes chronology, terminology, provenance, regional context, makers, materials and the documentary evidence surrounding an object or field.

Fieldwork places that evidence in the physical world: the shop, workshop, storage room, fair, collection, district, archive or property where the material actually exists.

Documentary photography and editorial work then create a coherent record rather than simply reproducing inventory.

Objects have provenance.
Businesses have histories.
Shops have worlds.
08 — Documentary Engagements

Some businesses contain a history that deserves more than a Trade Record.

The Trade Record establishes the reference point. Documentary work creates the permanent record.
ANTIQUE / Documentary Treatment

Your place in the larger trade.

A commissioned documentary treatment developed around a dealer's history, specialization, premises, collection, archive, family papers, material knowledge and place within the larger antiquarian trade. Research, interviews, fieldwork, photography, archival work and editorial development are scoped to the undertaking.

Dedicated Dealer Record

Your own documentary publication.

A separate commissioned documentary record devoted specifically to a dealer, specialist, collection, shop, family history or material field. The resulting publication can become a permanent institutional record of the business and the knowledge behind its work.

Documentary engagements are independently scoped according to the work required. Physical publication and print production are handled separately.
09 — Images & Material

The object is the evidence. The image makes the evidence visible.

ANTIQUE can work with properly licensed dealer-supplied imagery, archival material and original documentary photography produced through commissioned fieldwork. Images are selected and used editorially within the structure of the publication.

Dealers provide only imagery for which they hold or control the necessary rights. Image licensing is treated as a straightforward part of the publication process rather than as an assumption attached to ownership of the physical object.

Where original documentary photography is commissioned, the field record is developed around the premises, material, people, objects and processes that make the business distinctive.

Participation does not create a guaranteed advertising position or predetermined page treatment. The editorial structure of the annual determines how material is organized and presented.

The image is not decoration.
It is part of the record.
10 — The Book

Three volumes. One annual record.

The physical ANTIQUE publication brings together dealers, specialists, regions, material categories, shops, histories, documentary images and reference information into a coherent three-volume annual set.

The three volumes divide the enormous material field into logical areas without separating the trade into isolated worlds. Dealers are encountered through the material they handle, the specialties they practice and the places where they operate.

Geography, period and specialist expertise operate across the set. A dealer may therefore be understood not only by the volume in which the primary material field appears, but also through regional and specialist indexing.

The set is intended to be useful to the people already involved in this world: dealers, collectors, designers, architects, museums, libraries, researchers and institutions whose work depends on finding the people who know the material.

Each edition becomes a record of the trade at a particular moment: who was working, where they were working, what they knew and what material was moving through their hands.

Website Image Disclosure

The images presented on this website are not the photographic record of the ANTIQUE publication.

Website imagery may include original photography, hand-drawn and digitally constructed illustrations, photographic composites, interpretive reconstructions, publication mockups, and images created or modified with generative image tools. These visuals are used to establish atmosphere, subject, material context and the intended character of the publication.

Where appropriate, captions identify images as documentary photography, composite, illustration, reconstruction or mockup.

The ANTIQUE books are different. Images published in the physical set will be real photographic or archival images of actual objects, places, businesses and material evidence. Generative imagery will not be used as documentary evidence in the books.

The distinction is intentional: the website presents the project; the books record the material world.
