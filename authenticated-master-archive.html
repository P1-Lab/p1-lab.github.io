1. The Problem

Organizations accumulate photographs, video, scans, recordings, documents, and other original media. Over time they still have files. What they often no longer have is certainty about which files are authoritative.

The Accumulation Reality

Once the original files, devices, software, or institutional context disappear, reconstruction becomes difficult or impossible. Working copies proliferate. The master becomes ambiguous.

The Authenticated Master Archive creates a controlled, authenticated preservation copy of those assets and establishes it as the organization's authoritative master record.

2. How the Failure Happens

Nobody decides to destroy the archive. It happens through ordinary operations.

The Failure Usually Happens Quietly

A photographer leaves. A server is replaced. A production drive fails. A cloud account is closed. A publisher migrates DAM systems. A museum changes collection software.

A tourism organization discovers that its “original” photograph is actually a JPEG exported from a JPEG. A historic scan survives — but nobody can determine which version was the original.

The organization still has files. It has simply lost certainty about which files matter.

Preserve the original. Authenticate the master. Release copies.

3. Three-Step Preservation Protocol

Step 01
Ingest

Original media is collected without altering the source.

Step 02
Authenticate

Each master receives a Provision1 embedded authentication record. Cryptographic identity and preservation event are established.

Step 03
Preserve

The authenticated masters are placed into a controlled package designed to remain authoritative independently of ordinary production and distribution systems.

What You Keep

The organization owns the resulting archive and retains the authenticated master media.

Each master carries its Provision1 authentication record. The organization also receives an independent, air-gapped verification application capable of validating the authentication state of preserved assets without contacting Provision1 or relying on a cloud service.

No subscription. No proprietary cloud. No hosted verification dependency.

Each asset is independently verifiable. The customer does not need to contact Provision1 to verify an authenticated master.

The embedded authentication record is designed to remain recoverable from authenticated media even after ordinary downstream transformations such as compression, resizing, cropping, and format conversion.

5. What This Establishes

For every preserved master, the archive can establish:

  • This is the file that was deposited.
  • This is the exact cryptographic identity of that file.
  • This is what was known about its source at the time of preservation.
  • This is the preservation event that established the master record.

This establishes an authenticated preservation record. It does not, by itself, prove historical authorship or legal ownership.

Per-asset: The Provision1 embedded authentication record identifies the authenticated master.
Per-archive: The Archive-level Preservation Certificate documents the completed preservation event—date, identified assets, and procedure.

8. Why Now

Modern media increasingly passes through cameras, phones, editing systems, AI tools, DAMs, social platforms, cloud storage, and automated distribution systems. The original is getting harder to identify.

Capture Provenance vs. Master Preservation

Provenance technologies such as C2PA are making origin and modification history increasingly important. Capture provenance can tell you where a file came from.

Master preservation establishes which file your organization has chosen to preserve as authoritative.

As provenance standards mature for working assets, the absence of an institutional master becomes more visible—not less.
