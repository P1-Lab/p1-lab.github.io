BELOW Six films. Six hours. The world beneath Florida.

Florida looks different from below.

Beneath the familiar outline of the state is a world of thresholds and hidden passages: water rising through limestone, rivers carrying the interior toward the coast, mangrove roots sheltering life in their shade, seagrass moving with the tide, reefs gathering life in open water.

Florida Below follows these places from within, tracing what water makes possible—and what the wildlife living there reveals about the places themselves.

Filming planned · January–April 2027

The series / Natural history

Florida, seen
from within.

A spring can seem like a world of its own. So can a river, a stand of mangroves or a reef. But underwater, the boundaries are not so simple. Stone, current, light, temperature and tide set the terms; plants and animals find ways to live within them. Florida Below starts with each place as it is, then follows the connections that make it more than scenery.

Each film brings close observation together with scientific insight. The story may begin with an animal, a change in the water or the shape of a habitat—but it keeps asking what lies behind the visible moment, and what may change when the conditions change.

Editorial position / The approach

A place is never
just a place.

A limestone passage, a manatee in moving water, a maze of mangrove roots, a reef in the current: each belongs to a story larger than the frame. Florida Below lets the physical world lead, then follows the life that has found a way to live in it.

01 / Place and process

Begin with the place.

A spring is not just clear water; a river is not simply a route to the sea. Limestone, current, depth, shade and tide shape what each environment can support. The films begin with those physical details and follow where they lead.

02 / Animal lives

Watch what life does.

Where animals feed, shelter, travel or gather can tell us as much as a close portrait. Rather than treating wildlife as a series of spectacular appearances, the films look at behavior in context—and at the demands a place makes on the lives within it.

03 / Field observation

See first. Understand more.

Cinematography brings us close; patient field observation gives a moment time to unfold. Scientific expertise helps interpret what is seen without turning the film into a lecture. The aim is to leave the viewer with more to notice, not less room for wonder.

Each film has its own terrain and its own question. Together, they show a Florida that no single spring, river, shoreline or reef can explain on its own.

The environments / Six-film structure

Six environments.
One connected world.

The series moves from water emerging out of the ground to the shallows and reefs at Florida’s edge. Each environment brings its own physical character, wildlife and questions. Location choices, access and individual storylines will be established as the production is planned.

Film 01 / Freshwater origins

Springs

Water comes up through Florida’s limestone, bringing an unseen underground landscape into the open. Clarity, flow and temperature help determine what can live in these remarkable freshwater places.

Film 02 / Inland connections

Rivers

Rivers carry more than water. Rain, drought, flood, sediment and nutrients leave their mark as the current passes through different landscapes, linking habitats along the way.

Film 03 / The tidal threshold

Mangroves

At the meeting of land and sea, mangrove roots form a submerged maze. The incoming tide moves through it; fish, crabs and other animals use its cover, turning a tangle of roots into living space.

Film 04 / Shallow-water habitat

Seagrass Meadows

In sunlit shallows, seagrass beds provide feeding grounds and cover close to the seabed. Life moves through the blades and over the sand, while the condition of the meadow reflects the water surrounding it.

Film 05 / Offshore ecosystems

Reefs

Crevices, hard surfaces and moving water create a three-dimensional world where animals shelter, compete, feed and hunt. A reef’s story is written in the structure itself and in the lives that gather around it.

Film 06 / Changing underwater landscapes

Artificial Reefs

Human-made structures take on a different life beneath the surface. The questions are practical and ecological: which animals use them, how marine life gathers and changes, and what these built landscapes can—and cannot—tell us.

These six environments define the current programme framework. Final film treatments will establish each episode’s central question, principal storylines, scientific contributors and filming requirements.

Participation / Production partnerships

Put the right tools
in the water.

The production needs more than cameras. We are identifying partners whose imaging systems, underwater robotics, vessels, marine electronics, specialist equipment or field expertise can help us reach difficult places and record them well.

01 / Series-wide

Back the six-film series.

02 / Technology & equipment

Bring a capability to the field.

Underwater robotics, imaging systems, marine electronics, vessels and other equipment may give the production a way to reach a subject, hold a shot, work in difficult conditions or see what would otherwise remain out of view.

03 / Film-specific

Help solve a particular challenge.

A focused partnership tied to one environment, location or filming requirement—where a company’s equipment or expertise has a clear purpose in the work.

Depending on the agreed role, participation may include brand recognition, production support, selected original footage and stills, behind-the-scenes material, or licensed media for a partner’s own communications. Deliverables, exclusivity and usage rights are negotiated individually. Partners do not direct editorial decisions. Production / Editorial / Delivery

A clear point of view.
A practical plan.

P1Lab originated Florida Below and is developing the series, setting its editorial direction and bringing together the specialist capabilities needed to film it.

Each episode will begin with the character of its environment and what can be learned by spending time there. Field observation, relevant scientific expertise and location-specific planning will shape the stories, with filming designed around the conditions on site.

Filming is planned for January–April 2027. Site access, permits, technical requirements and local conditions will be confirmed as production planning advances.

Florida Below is intended for release through Florida Field & Ocean, P1Lab’s documentary programming initiative. Distribution arrangements and any partner use of footage or other materials will be agreed separately.

The documentary leads. Technology and expertise help us get closer to the story; they do not get to decide what the story is.
