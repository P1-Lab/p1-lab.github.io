FIELD / The Collection

Distinct worlds.
A record worth keeping.

FIELD documents material culture, currently centred on North America: the objects, makers, places, products, practices, and specialist knowledge that reveal how our world is made and understood. The geographic frame guides the collection without setting an absolute boundary; subjects elsewhere may be included when they make a distinctive contribution to its editorial purpose.

Each volume stands on its own as an inviting, carefully edited book. Together, the volumes build a coherent library—preserving a record of distinctive work and knowledge that might otherwise remain scattered, private, or undocumented.

FIELD treats material culture as living culture. A tool, workshop, nursery, boat, collection, or specialist practice can carry history and accumulated expertise. Our purpose is to look closely, establish context, and make that world accessible to readers.

One book opens a door. A collection opens a world.

Each volume offers entry into a particular world; the collection reveals the wider landscape of material life, making, and specialist knowledge.

The subject comes first. Where a business or maker is involved, the book documents what is distinctive and worth understanding—not simply what is being sold.

Design studies. P1Lab’s editorial design brings together two complementary traditions: the systematic grids of Swiss graphic design and the photograph-led storytelling of the great American picture magazines of the mid-twentieth century. Modular structure, disciplined typography, varied image scale, and carefully sequenced photography give each page a clear visual logic while allowing every subject its own character. The work draws on the editorial innovations of Alexey Brodovitch and Bradbury Thompson, alongside the design principles articulated by Josef Müller-Brockmann, Allen Hurlburt, and Emil Ruder.

FIELD / Why it looks this way

A print tradition,
made for now.

FIELD draws on the visual language of mid-to-late twentieth-century illustrated print: LIFE, TIME-LIFE books, National Geographic, and American Sunday magazine inserts. These publications made serious subjects accessible through vivid photography, confident headlines, useful captions, and a rhythm that invited browsing. FIELD applies that tradition to material culture: making the details of objects, places, processes, and expertise visible and understandable.

This is not nostalgia for its own sake. It is a way to document contemporary life with care: books that are rich without being crowded, informative without feeling encyclopedic, and considered without becoming austere. The visual experience should invite discovery while respecting the subject.

Collection participation

FIELD participation combines editorial selection with professional book production. Your subject becomes part of a curated series, represented in a finished publication that can be sold directly, given to clients and partners, shared with the press, and ordered through retail distribution.

The relationship is not confined to launch day. Documentary fieldwork

Some subjects require us to go there.

Some subjects cannot be understood from an existing archive alone. P1Lab undertakes deeper documentary work: on-site research, observation, interviews, photography, and the documentation of processes, materials, facilities, and place.

A Field Record may become the basis of a book or a broader documentary publication. Its purpose is to create a reliable, carefully shaped account of a subject whose details, methods, or knowledge deserve to be preserved—not left to memory or a passing description.

Our approach

Preservation begins with attention.

Every project begins with the subject and the reader: what is distinctive about this world, what is worth understanding, and what deserves to be recorded for the future.

We work from existing material wherever possible, then identify what additional research, interviews, or photography would add. The aim is a reliable, engaging record with a clear editorial point of view—not a sales page transferred to paper.
