Research & Publishing Series / Three Volumes

Pigments Trilogy

Hybrid Engine Topology, Micro-Granular Physics & Generative Sound Ecologies

An authoritative three-volume monograph series exploring hybrid engine topology, micro-granular metamaterials, and generative sound ecologies in Arturia Pigments.

Synthesizer software documentation often limits itself to interface navigation and preset scrolling. The Pigments Trilogy approaches Arturia Pigments as an advanced hybrid laboratory where multiple synthesis paradigms converge into a single architectural canvas.

By mapping the interactions between analog virtual emulation, wavetable morphing, sample granularization, and additive overtone matrices, this series establishes a rigorous framework for designing self-sustaining sound architectures.

The result is a comprehensive examination of multi-engine convergence: signal routing, non-linear filter cascading, asynchronous modulation, and generative patch design.
Governing Principle

“A multi-engine synthesizer is not simply a collection of independent sound sources; it is an interdependent ecosystem where phase, modulation, and spectral space constantly interact.”

01 / Architectural Convergence

Uniting discrete synthesis paradigms into one fluid canvas.

Arturia Pigments breaks down traditional walls between subtractive, wavetable, virtual analog, granular, and additive synthesis. The Trilogy provides the structural methodology required to master this multi-engine environment.

From Isolated Engines to Matrix Systems

Multi-engine interaction and signal flow topology.

True mastery of Pigments requires understanding how disparate engines feed into shared utility filters, wavefolders, and effect chains. The Trilogy analyzes the mathematical and perceptual consequences of running parallel versus serial signal paths across complex modulation sinks.

01 / Cross-Engine Modulation

Audio-rate cross-modulation and feedback loops.

Using engine outputs, function generators, and custom random generators to drive filter cutoffs, wavetable position indices, and granular density at audio rates.

02 / Spatial Metamaterials

Sculpting dimension through spectral and temporal effects.

Treating Pigments' onboard delay, reverb, distortion, and parametric processors as active acoustic shaping modules rather than static finishing tools.

02 / The Three Volumes

Three books. A complete mastery of the Pigments ecosystem.

A rigorous curriculum spanning engine foundations, granular metamaterials, advanced modulation matrices, and autonomous generative sound patches.

I

Pigments: Multi-Engine Topology & Granular Physics

Virtual Analog · Wavetable Synthesis · Filter Interlock · Utility Routing

Establishes the foundational mechanics of Engine 1 and Engine 2. Explores dual-filter topologies (Series, Parallel, Split), non-linear saturation staging, and the physics of wavetable morphing and phase distortion.

II

Pigments: Kinetic Granularity & Digital Metamaterials

Granular Engine · Harmonic Additive Engine · Spectral Diffusion · Time Manipulation

Focuses on time-domain distortion and particle manipulation. Investigates grain windowing, position scrambling, density modulation, and additive harmonic partial editing to create evolving synthetic textures and acoustic illusions.

III

Pigments: The Synthetic Ecology

Function Generators · Random Generators · Turing Logic · Autonomous Systems

Brings the engine systems to life through advanced modulation architecture. Explores multi-segment function generators, probabilistic random sources, complex scaling curves, and self-running generative soundscapes.

03 / Methodology & Approach

Rigorous analysis paired with clear structural execution.

Every section moves cleanly from acoustic principles to actionable architectural implementation within Arturia Pigments.

The architecture of sound design should be understood through its physical behavior, not rote button-pushing.

Rather than providing superficial preset guides, each volume breaks down complex synthesis interactions into observable components: harmonic partial distribution, transient envelope shaping, modulation depth ratios, and temporal decay behavior.

Technical mastery is achieved by examining how parameter changes alter the underlying mathematical and perceptual state of the sound.

Systematic Demonstration

Every core chapter includes targeted technical lab exercises demonstrating exact signal routing configurations.

Transferable Principles

Concepts learned within Arturia Pigments are framed to apply universally across advanced software and hardware modular environments.
