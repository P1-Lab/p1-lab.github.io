METHODOLOGY

PROVISION1 ATLAS RESEARCH STANDARD (PARS)

A systematic framework for discovering, validating, and mapping the unexplored capability space of complex human-technology systems.

THE CORE PROBLEM

Products Are Documented. Possibilities Are Not.

Manufacturers typically document specifications, performance characteristics, operating procedures, safety parameters, approved workflows, and intended applications. However, complex human-facing systems routinely contain capabilities that emerge only through rigorous experimentation, non-standard configurations, cross-disciplinary integration, and advanced user practice.

The difference is the difference between a list of applications and a map of possibility. A list tells someone that baking soda can clean, deodorize, or leaven. A capability map reveals the underlying properties that make entirely new applications possible.

The PARS Approach

PARS converts undocumented discovery into a structured, reproducible capability map. Every investigation moves systematically through five distinct operational phases:

  • 1. Capability Identification: Isolate the specific, previously undocumented behavior or capability vector being explored.
  • 2. Controlled Experimentation: Establish the precise environmental and system conditions under which the behavior emerges.
  • 3. Configuration Record: Document the exact hardware, software, environmental, and operational variables required to achieve the result.
  • 4. Technical Verification: Ensure findings remain technically verifiable, independently reproducible, and actionable across engineering, product, and creative teams.
  • 5. Capability Mapping: Assign the discovered behavior to its precise position within the broader operating envelope of the system.

The Atlas Output

The output of a PARS study is not a conventional report or user manual. The deliverable is a structured Capability Atlas: a codified repository of verified experiments, repeatable configurations, observed behaviors, and documented capability pathways. Each documented discovery becomes a reusable knowledge asset for end users, engineering teams, educators, and future product development.

Layer 1 — Known Territory

Established capabilities, documented factory uses, standard manufacturer guidance, and accepted operational workflows.

Layer 2 — Extended Territory

Advanced capabilities demonstrated by expert operators but poorly documented or absent from standard user materials.

Layer 3 — Adjacent Territory

New applications and operational behaviors revealed when the target system interacts with adjacent disciplines and tools.

Layer 4 — Frontier Territory

Unexplored capability vectors and edge conditions identified for future empirical research and product R&D.

The Experimental Record

Every PARS observation is recorded as a discrete research unit. Each unit captures Seven Core Data Domains:

1. Hypothesis

What specific capability or system behavior is being investigated?

2. Configuration

What exact system, signal, physical, and environmental parameters were established?

3. Method

What reproducible operational procedures were executed to induce the behavior?

4. Observation

What observable physical, signal, or computational outputs occurred?

5. Evidence

What calibrated physical data, audio/visual artifacts, or telemetry document the result?

6. Repeatability

Can an independent team reproduce the precise behavior using the documented configuration?

Who Uses a PARS Atlas?

Manufacturers understand how their products are designed and built. Users continuously discover what those products might become. PARS systematically connects the two.

Creators & Performers

Understand what a system can actually achieve without spending years discovering edge behaviors independently.

Product Development & R&D Teams

Identify missing ecosystem opportunities, accessory development vectors, and firmware updates. The Atlas reveals not only what a product can do today, but where future ecosystem expansion is most likely to emerge.

Engineers & Designers

Identify operational boundary conditions, mechanical or electrical integration limits, and cross-platform capabilities.

Educators & Technical Communicators

Construct rigorous, structured technical knowledge rather than relying on fragmented forum posts or isolated tips.

Inaugural Published Application

PROVISION1 ATLAS RESEARCH STANDARD — VOLUME 01

Expressive Technology Systems: Mapping the Modern Electric Instrument

The inaugural published PARS application demonstrates the methodology through an advanced expressive technology platform: the modern electric string instrument system. The objective is not product evaluation or comparison, but the construction of a reproducible capability map documenting the observable operating envelope of a complex human-technology system.

This investigation applies the PARS framework to expressive systems, documenting the interaction between physical technique, instrument architecture, electronic signal pathways, amplification systems, digital processing, and performer workflow.

The resulting Atlas demonstrates a transferable research model applicable across instruments, creative technologies, media systems, industrial tools, and other complex human-technology platforms where undocumented capability remains a barrier to adoption, education, and innovation.
