Florida Agriculture:
The Material Record

The agricultural world behind the visitor experience.

Palm Beach County is developing a new relationship between agriculture and tourism. P1Lab is documenting the agricultural world behind that emerging experience: land, crops, water, tools, machinery, nurseries, processing, buildings, working landscapes, histories, and the systems that make agricultural production possible.

For agricultural operators, landowners, nurseries, processors, suppliers, institutions, and other organizations with a stake in this world, the project also creates an opportunity to document places, machinery, processes, archives, company histories, and knowledge while the evidence and people who understand it are still available.

01 / The Record

The agricultural world is the subject. The institutions that hold it are part of the record.

Palm Beach County's emerging tourism strategy is making agriculture more visible as a visitor resource. This record begins one layer deeper: with the agricultural field itself and the organizations, places, systems, objects, and accumulated knowledge that make that field real.

Before the visitor sees the farm, there is a working agricultural system worth preserving.

The record follows the land, water, crops, tools, machinery, nurseries, packing, processing, buildings, roads, equipment, and other physical systems that make agriculture possible.

It also follows the places where agriculture becomes visible to the public: farm markets, working farms, plant nurseries, food systems, educational experiences, culinary connections, and other points where the agricultural field begins to meet the visitor economy.

For a participating organization, the value is not simply being named in a publication. The work can preserve a company's history, working environment, specialized machinery, processes, archives, photographs, and firsthand knowledge as part of a larger record of the world in which that organization operates.

The purpose is not to produce an agritourism guide or corporate brochure. It is to establish a material record of the agricultural systems that an emerging visitor economy is beginning to make more visible.

Documentary basis: Direct field observation, photography, firsthand interviews, historical material, maps, technical information, county planning documents, and other identifiable sources.

03 / Agri + Tourism

The emerging agricultural destination.

The visitor experience is becoming a new layer of the agricultural landscape. The record documents the agricultural world before, beneath, and beyond that layer.

The visitor experience begins where the agricultural system becomes visible.

Palm Beach County's current Tourism Master Plan identifies agritourism and rural tourism as an area for product development, including visitor-facing agricultural experiences and stronger connections between agriculture, food, rural communities, and tourism.

The plan's implementation framework begins with asset inventory and landscape study before moving into product development and technical support. Its stated performance target includes 25 new or enhanced agritourism businesses and experiences within three years.

P1Lab's role is different from destination marketing. This publication does not sell the visitor experience. It establishes an independent material record of the agricultural field from which that experience is emerging.

That distinction creates a useful point of connection for agricultural operators and institutions: the organizations that hold the land, machinery, expertise, archives, facilities, and working knowledge are also the organizations best positioned to help preserve the underlying record.

Current planning context: Palm Beach County's Tourism Master Plan identifies agritourism and rural tourism as an area for development and calls for an initial asset inventory and landscape study followed by product development and technical support. County plan

05 / Partners

Organizations can help make the record possible.

A partner may simply open the field and contribute knowledge. It may also commission documentation of its own operation, acquire an institutional edition, or preserve documentary materials created through the work.

Commissioned Documentation

Document your world.

Agricultural operators, landowners, nurseries, processors, suppliers, family enterprises, estates, institutions, and other organizations may commission a defined documentary undertaking around their own history, facilities, working processes, machinery, archives, landscape, or specialized knowledge. Scope may include field research, photography, interviews, archival capture, sound, moving image, and other documentary work where appropriate.

Institutional Acquisition

Preserve the record.

Finished books, institutional editions, documentary photographs, interviews, recordings, archival materials, and other agreed documentary outputs may be acquired for company archives, family records, executive presentation, education, exhibitions, training, customer or dealer relationships, and future research. Selected documentary assets may also be licensed for agreed institutional use.

A partner is not purchasing editorial placement. The commercial relationship supports, commissions, or acquires documentary work; the editorial record remains independent.
06 / Method

Document what can actually be established.

Palm Beach County is the geographic boundary. Access, field time, and the quality of the evidence determine the final record.

01 / Field

Go there.

Visit accessible agricultural environments and observe physical conditions, processes, materials, tools, crops, equipment, visitor interfaces, and spaces directly.

02 / Photograph

Show the evidence.

Photography establishes objects, details, sequences, landscapes, machinery, workspaces, materials, working conditions, and other visible conditions.

03 / Ask

Talk to people who know it.

Firsthand explanations from growers, workers, operators, processors, suppliers, tourism participants, historians, and others strengthen the documentary record.

04 / Restrain

Do not overstate the field.

The publication will not claim to represent every agricultural operation or every visitor experience in Palm Beach County. It will identify and document what can genuinely be established.

07 / Fieldwork

Palm Beach County first.

A county is manageable. Agriculture is not.

The project begins with Palm Beach County rather than attempting a statewide survey. The field will be developed through a selected number of accessible agricultural operations, material systems, historical sources, and emerging visitor-facing agricultural experiences.

Large-scale agriculture, horticulture, nurseries, processing, agricultural equipment, specialty production, markets, culinary connections, and smaller operations may all enter the record where access and documentary value make sustained observation possible.

Organizations may enter the project at different levels: by opening a facility, contributing historical evidence, making knowledgeable people available, commissioning documentation of a particular operation, or acquiring finished documentary work for institutional use.

08 / Institutional Memory

The present field still has a history worth capturing.

Agricultural history is often distributed across land, machinery, photographs, family papers, company records, buildings, manuals, and the memories of people who have worked there.

A working landscape carries institutional memory.

Historical photographs, maps, family archives, agricultural records, equipment histories, land-use changes, company histories, and firsthand recollections may enter the record where they help establish how the county's agricultural systems developed.

For a company or family enterprise, this same work can gather materials that otherwise remain scattered: the old machine in a shed, the photographs in an office, the story known by one retired specialist, the history of a facility, or the accumulated knowledge behind a process.

The result can become more than a publication. It can function as a durable institutional record—something to place in an archive, use in education or exhibitions, share with customers or future employees, and return to when the present has become history.

The objective is not nostalgia. It is continuity: establishing the material relationship between the agricultural county that existed, the agricultural county that remains, and the agricultural landscape increasingly being opened to visitors.
